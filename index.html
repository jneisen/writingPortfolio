Reflections

For the HBCU.edu assignment, I chose Talladega College, because the name reminded me of Talladega Nights: The Ballad of Ricky Bobby, a movie that is only related by location. I chose it because it is more interesting to write a paper about a unique website than one that blends in with the crowds. While writing the paper I compared it to Howard University’s website because that is the college website that I am most familiar with.

For my E-newsletter Assignment, I interviewed two of my fellow classmates in order to pick the story that I thought I could write better. In the first interview I wrote notes, and in the second I recorded it. When it came time to write about one of their stories, I remembered Mr. Swarmy’s interview went better and also found out that the recording that I created for the other interviewee was unlistenable because I didn’t have the microphone in the correct place. While writing the piece I noted that the questions that I had asked were not very good at exploring his philosophies. I wish that I would have curated better questions with which to ask him, because he seemed to be a genuinely interesting person. Reflecting on the article made me envy journalists who are much better at discovering the interesting quirks of people.

For the Internet Resource Guide, I had dreams of becoming a game developer, so that is the field I decided to write about. I mostly used resources in the guide that were interesting to me, as I have little experience in game development. Some of the sources could have used more picking and choosing, but I really don’t think that pre-compiled internet resources are that useful in the field, most of the techniques that you learn in game design come from searching out solutions to specific problems and interrogating why certain solutions are better or worse. While it stimulated my interest, I would be surprised if it is much use to anyone else.

For the job application package, I chose a position that paid well at a company that seemed to be putting good, well-intentioned products into the world. I don’t think that I would have even landed an interview with them on account of the main throughline of the application package being that I have no formal experience, but they might have appreciated the honesty. I still agree with highlighting my flexibility and excitement to learn.

I am a straightforward writer. My language is largely terse and uncreative, but effectively communicates the information that I need it to. The pieces that I most enjoy working on are the ones where I try and prove outlandish claims, because it helps me to see more nuance in the world. Over the semester I would say that I changed very little, largely because of my stubbornness. I am not one to change something that isn’t broken, and because the expectations for each assignment were clearly outlined and samples were provided, I could simply lean on my prior writing experience and style in order to mimic the samples. I see this as an unfortunate symptom of myself not really wanting to be a better writer. I write what is required of me, and while I wholeheartedly respect those who can cause deep sensation and thought, I currently have no intention of joining their ranks.

In terms of the usefulness of technical writing, this class opened my eyes to just how important presenting a good image to one’s employer is and how involved writing is in that perception. Most everyone that I have known presents their job as a necessary evil in their lives, but at the same time, they keep their jobs. This led me to believe that the image that one presents to an employer is not that important, but I realize now that no matter how you feel about a job, you have to do it with a smile on your face. Competency in output does not equal a job well done. During the class I also noted that most technical writers are not great writers, which makes me feel more confident that my writing skills are good enough. The job applications that I looked at didn’t require much in terms of writing, and seemed to weigh more heavily on the social skills or experience of candidates.

Finding Himself

Kai Swarny is a 20 year old Sophomore from Canton, Ohio pursuing a sociology degree at Howard University.

What is your family like? It’s just me, my mom, and my dog.

And what is your mom like? She is super sweet, supports me in everything I do, and someone that you could just talk to.

How has Howard University been for you? My first semester was a shock, I have never seen so many people in my life. The workload was a jump up from highschool, and I wasn’t entirely prepared, it led to me missing a lot of class last year.

What changes have you made in order to get yourself to class? I made a routine for myself and made sure I had something to do before class. My before class routine is to make my bed, get dressed, brush my teeth, do homework before getting to class, and I always leave some time to read also.

Why are you majoring in Sociology? I started in mechanical engineering - but it wasn’t really for me. I had never had to do math like that before in my life. I liked tinkering with things and putting them back together when I was younger, but mechanical engineering was so much more. Sociology, because of my interest in psychology, I wanted to find out more about how people interact with one and another and build social structures. Religion has always interested me, like learning about how people get their laws despite me not believing in them.

What is your favorite part of sociology? Researching different groups, finding out new concepts, and making connections between groups.

What are your goals for the future? Once I graduate, I wanna go to graduate school, and get a more research oriented degree. I want to make an impact, even if that is just making one thing better for some people.

And if you had to choose a single issue to focus on? Homelessness and hunger are the most important issues to me.

Do you have any plans of where to go for grad school? I want to go back to Ohio because it’s close to home, so I can be closer to my mom, but I haven’t looked too deep.
